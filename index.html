The early conversation with a broker should be ordered around the decision the borrower is actually making, not around a headline rate. The source guide frames the starting order as deposit size, repayment comfort, likely borrowing range, scheme rules, documents and the type of property being bought or refinanced. Rate comparison is only one part of loan fit

ASIC Moneysmart explains that home loan decisions include the loan amount, interest rate, fees, repayments and features. In the Ipswich context described by the source material, those basics still need to be tested against borrowing power, serviceability buffers, property type and the deadline for finance approval.

A borrower should ask for the comparison to show more than the lowest advertised rate. Useful questions include whether the repayment estimate stays comfortable if circumstances change, whether the product features match the borrower goal, whether refinancing costs are included, and what documents a lender expects before assessing the file. Investor and refinance conversations need particular care because the right answer depends on purpose. A lower rate may not be the whole answer if the borrower is releasing equity, changing features, consolidating debt or holding an interest only loan. Those points should be discussed as lending questions, not assumed from a calculator result.
